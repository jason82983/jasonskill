# Market Analysis Framework

Analyze each viable Niche across the same dimensions:

1. Demand: search volume, search conversion, trend history, persistence, and seasonality.
2. Supply: product count, seller count, brand count, Prime count, new products, and successful new products.
3. Concentration: Top 5/20 product click share and Top 5/20 brand click share.
4. Price: average price, main price bands, dispersion, premium acceptance, and common new-product pricing.
5. Product fit: target function, form, use scene, and keyword intent.
6. Consumer gap: repeated pain, return cause, unmet requirement, and willingness to pay.
7. Entry condition: differentiation, review barrier, advertising pressure, compliance, and supply-chain validation.

Interpret combinations instead of single-metric rules. A concentrated market may still admit a differentiated entrant; a fragmented market may still be unattractive if demand is weak or price is unsustainable. Without cost, FBA, advertising, and return inputs, describe price space only and do not claim true net profit.

## Development interpretation

Every material metric must answer what it means for improving the benchmark:

- High click concentration can indicate a strong mature solution and a harder acquisition path; check whether the improvement solves a repeated gap.
- New-product success is useful only when the time window and evidence support repeatability.
- Search volume is an entry-demand signal, not proof of market size or profit.
- Price dispersion can reveal positioning space, but a premium requires consumer and product evidence.
- Low concentration does not automatically mean opportunity if demand, conversion, or retention is weak.

## Market-universe interpretation

Before comparing market metrics, use the `所有细分市场` exports to establish which ASINs actually appear in which Niches. Keep the relationship map separate from the market verdict: an ASIN-Niche membership is a boundary fact, while Primary/Secondary/Overlapping/Adjacent/Weak is an analysis classification.

For each candidate Niche, combine these evidence dimensions:

| Dimension | Question | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Product fit | Does the product's function, form, and use scene match the Niche? | Product evidence + Niche product examples |
| Search intent | Are the main terms asking for the same job? | Niche search-term data |
| Market behavior | Are clicks/purchases and head products aligned? | Opportunity Explorer market/head-product data |
| Cross-ASIN overlap | Is the market shared by real competitors or only one ASIN? | Multiple dated `NichesProductAppears` exports |
| Market quality | Is demand, competition, price, and consumer evidence sufficient? | Candidate Niche detail folder |

Use qualitative `Strong / Medium / Weak / Unclear` evidence grades. Do not create a numeric score merely to force a Primary Market. If evidence is close enough to change the project decision, surface the conflict and use HOLD/STOP or request clarification.

## Dynamic leader rule

Leader is a dated research role, not an ASIN property. Accept it only from current Niche head-product or equivalent data that explicitly names the role. Membership-only rows are insufficient. Preserve historical and current observations separately when the role changes.
